Digital Storage Fundamentals

How each storage technology works, its strengths, weaknesses, and best use cases.

🖴

HDD — Hard Disk Drive

Mechanical storage using spinning magnetic platters and a read/write arm. Data is encoded magnetically. HDDs offer the highest capacity at the lowest cost but are slower and fragile due to moving parts. Best for bulk storage and backups.

Capacity500GB – 20TB
Speed80–160 MB/s
Cost/TB~$20
Moving PartsYes — fragile
⚡

SATA SSD

Solid State Drive using the SATA interface. No moving parts — data stored on NAND flash chips. Much faster, quieter and more durable than HDDs. SATA SSDs are the most common laptop upgrade. Speeds are limited to ~550 MB/s by the SATA interface.

Capacity120GB – 4TB
Speed~550 MB/s
Cost/TB~$60
InterfaceSATA III
🚀

NVMe SSD

Next-generation SSDs using NVMe protocol over PCIe, bypassing the SATA bottleneck. These are the fastest consumer storage devices. Reaching 7,000+ MB/s, they are used in gaming PCs, professional workstations, and the latest laptops. Come in the compact M.2 form factor.

Capacity250GB – 8TB
Speed3,500–7,000 MB/s
Cost/TB~$80
InterfacePCIe 4.0/5.0
🔌

USB Flash Drive

Small portable storage using flash memory in a USB form factor. No installation required — plug in and use. Great for transferring files between devices. Speeds vary dramatically: cheap USB 2.0 drives are 15 MB/s, while fast USB 3.2 Gen 2 drives exceed 400 MB/s.

Capacity4GB – 2TB
Speed15–400 MB/s
Cost/TB~$100
PortabilityExcellent
📸

Memory Cards (SD / microSD)

Tiny flash memory cards used in cameras, smartphones, drones, and gaming handhelds. SD card speed classes (UHS-I, UHS-II) determine recording performance. Essential for photographers needing fast portable storage in the field. MicroSD cards are used in phones and the Nintendo Switch.

Capacity2GB – 1TB
Speed10–300 MB/s
Cost/TB~$120
Form FactorTiny
💿

Optical Storage (CD / DVD / Blu-ray)

Data encoded on a spinning disc using a laser. CDs: 700MB, DVDs: 4.7–17GB, Blu-ray: 25–100GB. Optical media is exceptionally durable — archival-grade discs last 100+ years. Still used for movie distribution, software, and long-term data archiving where longevity matters most.

Capacity700MB – 100GB
Speed2–72 MB/s
Lifespan100+ years
Cost/disc$0.10–$2
☁️

Cloud Storage

Data stored on remote servers and accessed over the internet. Examples: Google Drive (15GB free), iCloud, OneDrive, Dropbox, AWS S3. Infinitely scalable, accessible from any device, and protected by built-in geographic redundancy. Speed depends entirely on your internet connection.

CapacityUnlimited
SpeedInternet dependent
Cost/TB/mo$2–$10
AccessibilityAny device
🔒

Data Security & Backup

Best practice is the 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies of data, on 2 different media types, with 1 copy offsite. AES-256 encryption scrambles data so only authorized users can read it. Regular automated backups protect against ransomware, hardware failure, theft, and accidental deletion.

Rule3-2-1 Backup
EncryptionAES-256
FrequencyDaily / Weekly
Test restoresMonthly
